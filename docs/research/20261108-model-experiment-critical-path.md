# Shortest defensible path to the next model experiment

November 8, 2026. This is an execution-priority recommendation grounded in existing repository receipts. It is not a new experiment registration, a protocol amendment, a source certification or authorization to open protected evidence. No new outcomes were inspected and no model was fitted in preparing this document.

The next work should resolve eligibility for one predictive comparison, rather than continue an open-ended series of locator improvements. Complete the current structural-consumer diagnostic, freeze its result even if negative, and move effort to historical source evidence and the specific reference review required for the proposed feature block. Full bill language remains a separate candidate information source; extracting a category does not replace it.

## What has already reached machine learning

The [exploratory calendar study](../operations/runs/20261108-exploratory-calendar.md) already executed nine actual model fits: three fixed arms in each of Congresses 115, 116 and 117. The evaluation population had 8,568 row appearances, 182 positive appearances and 7,596 distinct provisional global families. It was exposed development evidence, not independent validation.

The control's equal-period mean Brier was 0.01807882; time plus stage interaction achieved 0.01797026. This was a 0.6004% mean loss reduction against the control and 14.0822% skill against historical rate. The interaction worsened both Brier and log loss in Congress 115. Neither addition met the all-three-fold decision rule; both descriptive paired intervals included zero. The unchanged control remains the exploratory reference. These results are recorded outcomes of that study, not newly computed scores.

Its [separate protocol](EXPLORATORY_CALENDAR_PROTOCOL_V1.md) explicitly scoped an exception to human and positive-family computation gates for that nine-fit calendar diagnostic. It excludes new bill-text, policy, support and schedule features. That finite exception cannot be inherited by a text experiment, used to claim certification, or extended after seeing mixed results. A repeat without a new registered question supplies no new evidence.

## Smallest useful predictive contrast

Use one of the existing registered programme blocks once its prerequisites pass; do not combine all proposed feature ideas at once.

- **E2, mechanism/category information:** the fixed six-feature anchor versus that anchor plus accepted multi-label descriptors and explicit unknown indicators. Two arms per eligible fold, with the original three-fold plan totaling six fits. Both arms use exactly the same frozen core/calibration/evaluation memberships. A missing required fold prevents the all-fold lead decision.
- **E3, retained language:** the anchor versus anchor plus introduced text; the third combined-descriptor arm is conditional on E2 descriptor quality under the existing plan. Preserve the fixed TF-IDF and sparse-solver specification, core-only vocabulary/IDF and masking rules. Refit the structured control through the same solver. Do not add modern embeddings, infer arithmetic from fraction strings or convert proposed statutory deadlines into observed legislative schedules.

E2 is the smaller fit comparison, but it is not automatically the faster evidence path because semantic extraction certification is unresolved. E3 directly tests the user's full-language hypothesis; it still requires its source, measurement, family/time and numerical prerequisites. Decide the one block based on readiness before its scores, not by running both repeatedly and selecting the best result. The original 75-fit ceiling is an overall cap, not a reservoir of automatically authorized new trials. Each modeling block retains its six-hour computation ceiling, with a preserved failure/partial receipt at the cap rather than an outcome-dependent extension.

Core-only fitting of vocabulary, scaling and feature selection implements the train/test separation described in the official [scikit-learn common-pitfalls guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html#data-leakage), checked November 8, 2026. The guide explains that including evaluation data in learned preprocessing can bias measured performance. It supports this leakage precaution; it does not certify Momentum’s historical sources or establish its model’s efficacy.

The existing calendar exception does **not** make a new text ablation runnable now. If the team later wants computation before original certification, it needs a separate prospective protocol with explicit scope, disclosed unresolved historical evidence and a finite fit budget; it must not represent current renditions as proven historical inputs or call the result independent validation. Such a protocol would be a different diagnostic study, not completion of the original paper experiment. No such deviation is adopted here.

## What can run now without a predictive gate bypass

The current fixed-design structural extractor test, immutable-source replay, metadata/object evidence inspection, adversarial software fixtures and an outcome-blind source/row disposition ledger are legitimate immediate work. Existing calendar results and their saved predictions may be verified within their registered scope; this does not authorize new arms or tuning. Synthetic numerical fixtures can validate a future sparse solver without learning from bill outcomes. These activities should each close a named prerequisite rather than generate an endless chain of general-purpose infrastructure.

Do not begin the new predictive fit merely because the 40-case design extractor reports no crashes, preserves offsets or intersects more quotations. These diagnostics answer different questions from whether probabilities improve.

## Decision and eventual improvement claim

After this structural run, prioritize one sealed evidence-readiness decision for one matched feature comparison. Do not make another round of category/regex expansion the default next task. Keep full text and structure available, but test their incremental predictive value separately from extraction engineering quality.

The 60% objective remains `1 − mean-period(Brier_model) / mean-period(Brier_rate) ≥ 0.60`, with the historical-rate baseline fitted only on eligible earlier data and identical evaluation cases. It is neither 60 percentage points of accuracy nor a promise of improvement. Before any protected outcome access, a specific 60% claim needs the additional prespecified `mean-period(Brier_model − 0.40 × Brier_rate)` contrast and simultaneous interval/multiplicity accounting described in the recovery plan. Report rate and stage baselines separately.

Model learning means fitting new forecasting weights to genuine, matured labels under a fixed versioned procedure. Automatic self-labels, agent agreement, unmatured negatives and repeated selection on exposed outcomes do not supply independent learning evidence. The existing controlled-updating plan uses completed, adjudicated Congress boundaries and separate immutable forecasts; no self-updating model or deployment is introduced by this work.
