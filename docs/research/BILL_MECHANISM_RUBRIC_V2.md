# Bill mechanism and direction rubric v2

November 8, 2026. Status: design-only annotation specification. Supersedes ambiguous v1 category interpretation for a **new** 40-case design batch, without rewriting any v1 labels or touching quality cases. No outcome evidence informs this rubric. Agent review is not human certification or historically eligible model input.

## Unit and representation

Retain full introduced bill language and its exact source/view hashes. Record each supported operative effect as `mechanism`, `operation`, `dimension`, `actor`, `target`, `conditions`, `quote`, `start`, `end`, `rationale`. Quote must be an exact contiguous substring of the supplied reading view; record the selected occurrence's exact character offsets (`start` inclusive, `end` exclusive). If the same quote occurs more than once, select the operative occurrence after checking surrounding text; never silently choose the first. Include enough quoted clause context to preserve negation, conditions and operative scope. An actor or condition not supplied is null, never invented. Each effect can use overlapping mechanisms if each meets its own definition.

A bill also has a status for each of twelve mechanisms: `present` when at least one supported operative effect exists, `absent` when full supplied text contains none under this rubric, `uncertain` when a concrete unresolved interpretation/source dependency prevents a decision. Every uncertain mechanism needs a specific rationale. If at least one supported effect exists, the mechanism status remains present; record any additional unresolved effects separately in `unresolved_effects` rather than erasing supported evidence. Any present mechanism requires at least one evidence-backed effect. Presence means **the bill operates on that mechanism in a stated direction**, not that it necessarily creates or strengthens it. Thus waiving a deadline is `deadline/present` plus `waive`, rather than a positive imposed-deadline flag. This deliberate change makes v2 statuses noncomparable to v1 agreement rates or labels without a mapping study.

Each effect uses one operation from: `create`, `expand`, `restrict`, `remove`, `waive`, `replace`, `preserve`, `rename`, `clarify`, `uncertain`. These describe only an operation visible in supplied text, not the net impact relative to unavailable law. Every effect also declares a dimension: `coverage`, `amount`, `duration`, `authority_scope`, `procedure`, `name`, `existence`, `text`, or `other`. Expand/restrict are relative to the named mechanism and dimension, never a global pro-/anti-regulation judgment: expanding prohibition coverage bars more conduct; removing a prohibition is remove, not expand. Changing a time window must explicitly name that window and use duration. Rationale states which actor/target is affected. Use `uncertain` when mechanism involvement is clear but direction is not. `replace` records substitution without presuming expansion or restriction. `preserve` requires an operative savings/construction clause; background descriptions do not qualify. Use preserve for explicit savings/nonconstruction clauses that retain an option/protection; use clarify for an explicit definition or scope clarification without a visible addition/removal of the mechanism. Summaries describe only proposals visible in the text, not enactment, net historical impact, or unstated prior law.

## Mechanism definitions and exclusions

| Mechanism key | Included operative treatment | Explicit exclusions / boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `create_programme` | Establishment, substantive change or termination of a named programme, fund, institutional framework or entity; retained key denotes programme treatment in v2 | Merely mentioning an existing agency, assigning one study, a definition, or a generic payment-process change without programme structure |
| `authorize` | Explicit conferral, preservation, withdrawal or conditioning of legal discretion/power/permission, including a discretionary option within a mandated process; explicit authorization of future appropriations | Tax eligibility alone; statements of existing powers in findings; an affirmative duty alone; direct money provision without a distinct authorization clause |
| `appropriate` | Making specified money available, rescinding it or changing an actual appropriation's availability | Authorization to appropriate in a later act; estimates, ceilings, fees or cost descriptions without an operative appropriation |
| `tax` | Operative change to a tax, credit, deduction, exclusion, tax base, rate, exemption or tax administration | Merely citing a tax statute unrelated to an operative tax treatment |
| `prohibit` | Express bar, limitation or removal of a bar on conduct or legal powers; explicit scoped denial of reliance on a power | Arithmetic exclusions/denial of a deduction are tax treatment only unless a separate conduct bar exists; “shall not be construed to prohibit X” preserves authority to X, not a prohibition of X; mere nonapplication of an unseen referenced clause is uncertain |
| `require` | Affirmative mandatory conduct, payment, disclosure or certification, including a conditional duty when its trigger occurs | Exposure to alternative sanctions alone; permission to act; findings. A reporting obligation may also be require if both definitions are met |
| `amend` | Explicit addition, deletion, redesignation or replacement of statutory/regulatory text | Do not infer substantive repeal, expansion or restriction from deletion of unseen words. Amendment direction can be replace/remove without knowing net effect |
| `repeal` | Explicit repeal of a whole identified provision; explicit wholesale striking of a section/subsection; explicit abrogation of a named doctrine/defense | Replacing text or deleting isolated words/clauses is not automatically repeal. If whether the whole provision is removed cannot be resolved, uncertain. Abrogation concerns doctrine, not necessarily statutory repeal; name that target |
| `rename_designate` | Operative renaming of an existing public entity/facility or conferral/removal of a substantive legal designation/status | Routine appointment of a payee, clause renumbering, section-header editing, naming a newly created fund without a separate status designation, or merely authorizing a future honor |
| `reporting` | Operative report, notification, disclosure, certification or information-submission duty and its amendment/removal | Mere collection/study with no communication requirement; title or findings containing “report” |
| `deadline` | Due-by, within-period or event-triggered completion/submission requirement; changing/removing/waiving such a rule | Effective dates, eligibility dates, spending-year coverage and quantity-duration caps alone are not action deadlines. A certification explicitly due with each registration qualifies; seven-day medicine supply alone does not |
| `sunset` | Expiry/termination or extension/removal of an expiry of a substantive programme, legal authority or waiver; contingent expiry qualifies when the terminating condition is stated | Authorization fiscal-year ranges alone; a deadline for an action; quantity-duration cap. External expiry language not supplied is uncertain, not invented |

## Interpretation safeguards

Use operative clauses, including clearly inserted operative statutory text. Findings, titles, examples, quotations of language being struck and references to other law require context; they are not automatically effects. “Not” changes scope. “May not” usually restricts; “may” usually grants discretion; neither is a universal keyword rule. Do not claim a bill appropriates funds merely because “appropriations” appears.

A full bill may affect several mechanisms and directions. Record distinct effects where meaningful; do not collapse “remove limitation” and “impose limitation” into the same numeric flag. For legislation amending law, preserve exactly what can be observed; unresolved displaced-law dependencies belong in uncertainty notes. No external lookup, later outcomes, current political information or quality-case text may be used in this design batch.

## Fixed application and review

Apply once to the remaining 40 original design cases, excluding all ten v1 semantic cases. Split workload deterministically by Congress: first four remaining cases to agent A, last four to agent B, eight cases per Congress overall. Each agent reads its 20 full texts and seals its primary annotations before seeing the other batch. The agents then cross-review each other's complete batch against the same fixed rubric, reporting supported items, requested corrections and unresolved cases. This is **one primary annotation plus peer challenge per case**, not two blinded annotations or an agreement/accuracy experiment.

A third methods agent reviews this rubric before freeze and independently audits a deterministic ten-case subset after application (first two remaining cases per Congress), plus all peer-requested corrections. Do not cherry-pick only easy or agreed cases. New versions/notes append; original annotations remain immutable. Any desired rubric changes are recorded for a later version, not silently applied after reading this batch.

Outputs: 40 bill summaries; 480 mechanism-status assessments; supported action records with exact quotes and direction; explicit unknowns; primary and peer receipts; independent source-subset audit; counts and readiness limits. Validators certify structure/source matching only. They cannot certify interpretation, historical availability, family genealogy or lack of pretrained knowledge.

No fits, forecasts, outcome scoring, quality-set extraction or source acquisition occur in this batch. Do not derive precision/recall or prediction gain from peer review. A later predictive study requires a frozen encoding, a reviewed extractor, source coverage and separately registered matched populations.
