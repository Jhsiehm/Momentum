## Source alternatives and stopping decisions

1. **Historical recovery:** use a bounded, preregistered route for the same eight packages that adds evidence beyond the exhausted current PREMIS/ZIP requests. An older object identifier, historical package date, newly downloaded PDF or matching present-day XML alone does not close the chain. Preserve unsuccessful responses and partial evidence. Do not select replacement cases because they look easier.
2. **Custodian evidence:** the prepared local request specifies bytes/fixity, historical public rendition, transformation provenance and reproducible retrieval. It remains unsent unless outbound contact is explicitly authorized; no permission is needed to finish local preparation. A response still needs verification and designated review.
3. **Prospective collection:** a new registered collector can retain actually observed official bytes and retrieval records without claiming they existed before observation. Revisions and failures are append-only. This starts a usable evidence trail for later cutoffs; it does not issue a forecast, supply matured labels, resolve historical folds or satisfy the paper's separate protected prospective study automatically.
4. **A different limited diagnostic:** any proposal using current renditions without established historical availability must be separately scoped and preregistered, explicitly disclaim historical forecasting validity, and not inherit the calendar exception. This review does not adopt or execute such a deviation.

For any later prospective evaluation, specify eligible population, prediction checkpoint, at-risk checks, training-label cutoff, frozen model/calibration, outcome horizon and complete negative-label coverage before outcomes. The original paper additionally requires its exact day-180 issuance contract, independent timestamp/custody and designated future periods; a generic acquisition utility cannot silently activate those requirements. No automatic self-updating or promotion is justified by collection alone.
